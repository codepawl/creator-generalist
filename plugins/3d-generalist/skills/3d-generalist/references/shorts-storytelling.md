## 1. Premise selection (what gets 20M vs what flops at 20k)

- **Start from a mess or everyday thing everyone knows, end on a basic human need:**
  - *Hits (1M to 20M views):* Muddy Puddle -> Village Well, Snow -> Igloo, Hole in the Ground -> Bunker / Skyscraper,
    Box on a Rope -> Elevator, Staircase -> Escalator, Porta-Potty in a Storm.
  - *Flops (16k to 54k views):* Retractable Stadium Pitch, Airplane Door, Wood Block -> Car Brakes, Seabed Trench.
- **Literal transformation:** the starting object or mess must literally become the finished structure step by step in the
  same set. When a small object inspires a big one (a paper pinwheel -> windmill sails), say the link explicitly in the
  voice (*"you take the pinwheel's idea and build a bigger one"*) and build the transition across 2 to 3 beats, never
  drop the whole finished building onto the ground in one sentence.
- **Consistency wins:** keep one visual format, one recurring character, one steady camera style and a regular cadence.
  A channel that drifted between formats lost its audience even though viewers liked each video.
- **Everyone touches it weekly:** drop a topic that needs a niche fact to matter. In this format, hits got 1M to 2M views;
  stretched picks (a lawn becoming a moving stadium pitch) fell to 16k to 54k though viewers liked them as much.
- **Pick a bodily, daily-life need:** water, smoke, heat, food going bad, floods. Both breakouts opened on a need said to
  "you" ("If you're thirsty"); the flops opened on an object. Add one playful or odd real fact near the end that people
  want to comment on (never an accident or a stat list), and a title pun people can play with.
- **Retention is the first gate:** check "stayed to watch" at about 48 h. Under about 65% the feed stops serving the
  Short; above it is necessary, not sufficient (a 75% Short with an object opener still stalled at 3k). Sourced, plausible
  numbers matter: viewers check every weight and size in the comments.

## 2. The script recipe (40 to 45 s, 60 s at most)

Length (latest target, replacing 60 s): 40 to 45 s, about 150 words; under 40 s or over 45 s fails the gate, and 41.7 s felt
rushed (aim for 45). Get there with pace, never by cutting a problem, fix, flip or payoff: tighter gaps, faster actions,
time-lapse for repeated pieces, a faster voice where it still sounds natural. Fewer rounds fit than at 60 s. The older
60 s recipe below (about 230 words at 3.4 to 3.6 words/s) is the ceiling.

Write `script.md` as a beat table (`| id | role (hook/hold/close) | narration | picture |`) before touching 3D code:

1. **Second 0 Hook (0 to 3 s):** open on the naive first build or immediate danger in one sentence while the picture is
   already moving. No intro, no title card. Example: *"If you dig a hole and put a roof over it, you have a bunker."*
2. **5 to 7 rounds of `"But <real failure>. So <one fix>."` (~7 to 9 s per round):**
   - Show the physical failure first (2 to 4 s: it floods, collapses, fills with smoke, tips over).
   - Build the fix piece by piece in place (4 to 6 s) as a real builder would (if well water is contaminated by dung,
     dig a new well uphill rather than patching polluted water).
3. **Weave technical terms into the sentence:** say *"You build a **cold trap** to..."* or *"You need a **gooseneck**..."*.
   Never say *"This is a <term>"* (sounds childish). Teach **one named part per round**, with at most 1 or 2 plausible,
   sourced numbers in the voice (viewers fact-check weights and sizes in the comments).
4. **One real-life `"that is why"` fact:** connect a part to something viewers see every week (*"that is why pipes in
   fields are bent like candy canes"*).
5. **The Flip:** late in the build, a problem turns out to be the fix (melting inside walls refreeze into an airtight
   ice shell; blast pressure pushes the bunker door tighter; trapped air pockets in snow insulate the dome). When the
   twist is about something tiny (trapped air pockets), fill the visible face with glowing pockets popping in one by one.
6. **Final stress test:** throw a storm, 3-day blizzard, depth pressure or drought at the finished build and show it
   working while the character stays safe and comfortable inside.
7. **Closer + Meme Dance Outro (under 3 s):**
   - Close with a clean result + payoff line (*"Put it all together, and you have a <thing>."*), followed by a short ask
     (*"Like and subscribe to see what we build next"*).
   - Pop in 3D/2D Like & Subscribe buttons while the character performs a celebration meme dance (such as Gangnam Style)
     with its instrumental riff playing quietly (-2 dB under the main music, panned and low-passed to the character's
     position on screen while the main music keeps playing).

## 2b. A story, not a list of build steps

Measured on 1,960 top science-explainer Shorts with view counts (median 25M): openings with "if you / when you" have a
28M median against 24M, a "you might think / most people think" line 28M against 25M, a question title 28M against 25M,
and third-person "He / She / They" openings only 22M against 26M.

- **Line 1 puts the viewer in trouble** ("If you're...", "Imagine you...") with a cost the body feels: hunger, smoke in
  the eyes, a flooded bed. Never open on a neutral object ("A paper pinwheel is a tiny windmill" drew 295 views; "If
  you're thirsty..." drew 44k on the same channel the same week).
- **Open one question early and answer it late:** a wrong belief ("most people would just..."), or a tease of the flip
  ("what finally fixes it is the stuff you throw away"). Pay it off in the last third, never sooner.
- **The character wants something and pays for each failure** (coughs, burns a hand, loses the dinner). Every "But" is
  worse than the round before, not just different.
- **Break one expectation** mid-video ("you'd think a bigger fire helps, but it eats twice the wood").
- **Picture and voice carry different things:** the picture shows the failure a beat before the voice names it, or a
  small gag the voice ignores.
- **Cut lines that only list parts or materials.** Each line adds a problem, a fix, a reason or a surprise.
- **End where it began:** the last image echoes the first so the loop invites a rewatch.
- **Give the comments one hook:** a pun, a claim people argue with, a number they will check.
- **Title with a small story:** put the viewer in it ("in your hand"), say what is wrong with the start ("a bad
  design hut"), or give the start a job ("your wet cloth helps build"). A bare "How a X Becomes a Y" reads empty.
- Read the script aloud as a stranger: if any 5 s holds no new question or surprise, rewrite it.
- **Every "so" says its reason first.** A script that jumped from "water rolls off" to "so you take the ash" left viewers
  lost: it needs "you need soap" and "ash water turns fat into soap" between. Read the first three beats as a viewer who
  knows nothing; if a step needs a guess, add its line, even past the length target. Fast means quick delivery, not
  dropped information.
- **Name the link when the start object inspires the end one**, and build the jump over 2 to 3 beats, never the whole
  finished structure in one line ("you take the pinwheel's idea and build a bigger one").
- **Show the flip, never announce it:** no "here's the flip", "plot twist" or "here's the trick". Say it plainly ("the
  carts that made the rut now build the road").
- **A named idea gets the why before the spec:** what failed, what was changed, how it works, then the one number. A bare
  "small broken stone, none over six ounces" left viewers lost.
- **Never pad time:** a line that recounts a wait ("six months later...") goes straight to the result, and the picture never
  holds still for more than about 1 s.
- **Fix a failure the way people really would:** ask what a real builder does before writing the "So..." line (dirty well
  water: dig a new well elsewhere, never patch the polluted one).
- **Fact-check before the build:** list sources at the top of `script.md`, cover the voice, numbers, dates, how the process
  works and the period's tools and materials, mark each claim verified or unsure, and cut or soften the unsure ones.

## 2d. Writing the voice to be spoken

- **Write for the ear, not the page.** Talk to "you" the way a person tells it: contractions, joining words ("so", "and
  now", "funny thing is"), a comma where a speaker breathes, numbers and years as said ("seventeen-fifty"), an odd word
  explained in passing ("tallow, which is just beef fat"). Chain steps to reasons with plain joiners (So, Then, Now,
  Because, Meaning, until) and give the mechanism a whole sentence, never a noun list.
- **No colons, semicolons, dashes, brackets or note-form fragments:** the voice model reads them flat or pauses wrong.
- **Concrete comparisons a child knows** ("like a plunger"); end on the result in one plain line, often with a small
  surprise, never setup words like "here's the thing".
- **One steady voice and tone for the whole Short.** Do not use audio tags. Never print the API key.

## 2c. Narrated 30 s fact Shorts: what the views measured

Measured on 2,589 narrated 3D fact Shorts (about 85 words, 30 s each), then re-tested on 209 with upload dates.

- **Age decides most of the gap.** Median views by upload year: 2022-2024 about 28M, 2025 20.5M, 2026 17M. Length,
  words per minute, title words, opener grammar and topic (13 of 14 classes between 17.5M and 23M) hardly move views.
- **No script feature held up in recent videos.** Inside 2025-2026 uploads, every opener rule came out flat:
  hypothetical opener 1.00x, "you" in the first 25 words 1.00x, a "most people think" line in the first 40 words 0.97x,
  past-tense recount opener 0.92x. Older uploads showed a hypothetical opener at 1.40x and "you" at 1.13x, which may be
  a past fashion or survivorship.
- **So treat these as craft defaults, not view levers:** open on "what happens if" with the viewer's body in it, name
  a wrong belief and break it early, avoid a past-tense recount and an imperative recipe as the first line. They read
  well and cost nothing, but do not promise more views. Imperative openers even measured 1.36x in new videos (8 videos).
- **Do not tune what did not measure in any band:** twist position (first turn word at about 25% of the script),
  digits, gross words, scale comparisons, closer type and length.
- **What does move views is outside the script:** posting into a live trend, the premise's novelty, and the picture.
  Test script choices on our own channels with paired uploads instead of copying corpus averages.

## 3. On-screen UI, 3D labels, captions and sound

- **3D leader-line part chips only:** when the voice names a technical part (`ADOBE`, `COLD TRAP`, `U-BEND`), extend a
  3D leader line from the object to a small label panel sitting next to it in 3D space:
  - *Primary / next part:* solid line, dark strong colour.
  - *Secondary / previous part:* dashed line, light colour.
  - Fade and shrink the leader line back into the dot **1.5 to 2 s after the word** is spoken.
  - **No number chips, date chips, place chips or final-object chips:** never float `"62 BC"`, `"2 M"` or `"IGLOO"`
    cards on screen; let the voice and captions carry numbers and plain words.
- **Side gauges only when a changing number is the point:** bring a temperature or depth bar in with motion only for the
  beat where the mercury or level climbs into the red/yellow striped danger zone, then animate it out.
- **One-word centred captions (`~80 px` at `1080` width, `9 px` stroke):** display one word at a time at `Y = 1010`,
  synced within **±0.05 s** of the spoken word start (verified by ASR transcription against `timing.json`).
- **Sound carries 50% of realism:** no silence over `0.3 s` in the voice (`trim_tail` cuts beat tails 0.12 s after the
  last sound above -40 dB). Layer soft, real foley cues (stone set, gravel dig, water splash, wood knock, wind gust,
  fire crackle) for every visible action, mixed at **-14 LUFS / -1 dBTP** with music 10 dB under the voice. Drop
  continuous sandy footsteps that sound like white-noise hiss.
- **Repeated sounds vary:** a run of the same action (blocks set, pegs tapped, salt thrown) gets soft short pops that
  change pitch and level a little each time. One loud sample repeated sounds like a machine.
- **Loudness order: voice, then the main SFX (the story moments the voice names: pours, fire lit, the like button), then
  foley (handling: lifts, set-downs, stirs, drips).** Render them as two stems and keep foley about 10 LU under the
  main SFX; a mix checker's "quiet cue" flag on foley is expected, so never raise foley to clear it. Report the gap
  with every preview. Weather (a blizzard, a gust, nightfall) is an audible cue over the music, not a bed at -30 dB.
- **Build the sound once per episode.** A new picture round reuses it and shifts only the cues whose action moved; render
  every stem from its current event list, or an old cue (a wave crash) plays over a later cut. Rejected on the pilot:
  cartoon sounds (slide whistle, boing) and meme stings.
- **Captions move off the action:** where hands and props sit at frame centre (kneeling work filmed from above), drop the
  word onto empty ground (about 1460 px at 1080 x 1920). ASR word starts jump 0.04 to 0.08 s between runs: refine once toward the
  mean of three transcriptions (two muxed runs and the clean voice), then stop and report the words still outside.
- **Show what the camera cannot see:** a smell, heat or cold is a problem the viewer cannot see. Show it with a soft
  visual (faint green wisps and a few flies for rot, shimmer for heat, breath fog and frost for cold) and the
  character's reaction (pinches the nose, wipes the brow, rubs the arms).
- **No dead air in the picture either:** a Short never holds a still, empty frame. If the voice pauses, something on
  screen still moves: the character works, reacts or walks, or the camera moves.

## 4. Packaging: title, thumbnail, description, tags

- **Title:** names the start object and the end thing in the words people type ("stone arch bridge") and carries a small
  story: put the viewer in it ("in your hand"), say what is wrong with the start ("a bad design hut"), or give the start a
  job ("your wet cloth helps build"). No dash or colon; a bare "How a X Becomes a Y" reads empty.
- **Thumbnail:** a frame of the film itself, never a designed card. Pick the moment a stranger gets at phone size (the
  worker doing something, the build recognisable) from about 10 candidate seconds, read as frames.
- **Description:** one or two plain sentences, the first using the main search phrase and saying what the video really
  shows; then one credits line with creator names only; then `#shorts` and 2 or 3 tags. Licence text stays in the
  project's credits file.
- **Tags:** 8 to 12 specific phrases people search (the thing, its parts, "how X works", "how to build X"), never filler
  such as "amazing". Write it all with `write-clear-prose`: no sales words, no stacked emojis.
- A Short that is entertainment first with learning on top files under the entertainment category; the learning side
  lives in tags and description.
